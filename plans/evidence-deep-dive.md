# Evidence deep dive

Take the lotml study from "claims checked against a source" to "claims checked by a machine": read
the primary sources in full, pin every numeric claim to a verbatim quote, settle by experiment
the questions the first study left as inference, and turn the result into a complete roadmap
plan.

## Why

The first study (plans/llm-language-study.md) cites about fifty sources and leaves several
statements flagged as inference or "not measured": that a Python AST with lotml positions makes
tracebacks point at the `.lotml` file, the cost of trapping overflow on the Python target, the
sample size the harness needs, whether significant indentation is riskier than braces under edits
(the condition in adr:0005-significant-indentation), whether the grammar can constrain generation
on the APIs that accept grammars, and whether value semantics really breaks the code the pilot's
smallest model wrote. Done when each of those has a reproducible result in `research/`, every
numeric claim the decisions rest on is backed by a quote checked against the downloaded paper, and
the roadmap plan cites that evidence.

## Out of scope

- Implementing the lotml compiler: the transpiler written here is research code for the pilot's
  subset, not the phase 1 transpiler.
- Redistributing papers: the PDFs are downloaded to a git-ignored directory; only identifiers,
  checksums and quotes are committed.
- Paid APIs, including Anthropic's `count_tokens`.
